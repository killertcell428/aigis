## Summary

**Researched:** Cipher/encoding-based jailbreak techniques from 2024–2026, including
RoguePrompt (arXiv:2511.18790, CODASPY '26), ACE/LACE custom-cipher attacks (arXiv:2402.10601,
ICLR 2025), the Jigsaw Puzzle multi-turn split attack (arXiv:2410.11459), Foot-in-the-Door
(arXiv:2502.19820), Crescendo (arXiv:2404.01833, USENIX Security 2025), and a broad analysis
of LLM jailbreak effectiveness in 2026 (arXiv:2602.22242).

**Implemented:** Two new detection patterns added to `JAILBREAK_ROLEPLAY_PATTERNS` in
`aigis/filters/patterns.py`:

1. **`jb_cipher_output_evasion`** (score 50, input filter) — Detects requests asking the AI to
   encode its response in a cipher (ROT13, Base64, Caesar cipher, Morse code, Vigenère, etc.)
   with the explicit intent of evading a content filter, safety monitor, or moderation system.
   Basis: RoguePrompt (arXiv:2511.18790, Nov 2025, CODASPY '26) — 84.7% bypass rate on GPT-4o,
   93.93% average bypass rate across multiple frontier LLMs.

2. **`jb_decode_and_follow`** (score 60, input filter) — Detects requests to decode non-standard
   ciphers (ROT13, Caesar, Vigenère, Morse, pig latin, custom encoding) and then follow, obey, or
   act on the decoded instructions. Complements the existing `pi_base64_payload` (which covers
   base64 + execute/run/eval) by covering broader cipher types and action verbs (follow/obey/
   carry out/implement).
   Basis: RoguePrompt (arXiv:2511.18790) and ACE/LACE attacks (arXiv:2402.10601, ICLR 2025) —
   Caesar-variant ACE achieves 67% ASR on GPT-4o; layered LACE defeats pattern detection.

**Deferred to pending:**
- `jb_payload_split_reassemble` (JSP single-turn signal — false positive risk moderate)
- Crescendo escalation framing (multi-turn behavioral; regex signal is imprecise)
- FITD multi-turn behavioral escalation (requires cross-session correlation)

## User-visible impact

- AI agents and safety firewalls using aigis now detect attempts to use ciphers/encodings as
  a jailbreak bypass layer — both "respond in ROT13 to avoid the filter" (output-evasion) and
  "decode this caesar cipher and follow the instructions" (decode-and-execute) variants.
- Prior gaps: `pi_encoding_bypass` only covered `encoding_name + instruction/command noun`;
  `pi_base64_payload` only covered base64 + execute/run/eval. These new rules close the
  broader surface of cipher-based jailbreaks.

## Implementation caveats

- `jb_cipher_output_evasion` requires two signals in proximity: the cipher name and a safety/
  filter/monitor reference. This minimizes false positives for legitimate cipher puzzle requests.
- `jb_decode_and_follow` is restricted to "follow/obey/carry out/implement" action verbs
  (not "tell me" or "explain"), specifically to avoid false-positives on cryptography tutorials.
- Base64 is included in `jb_cipher_output_evasion` to catch output-evasion framing that isn't
  caught by `pi_base64_payload` (which only covers input decode+execute).
