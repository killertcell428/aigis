## Summary

Added three new jailbreak detection patterns to `JAILBREAK_ROLEPLAY_PATTERNS` in `aigis/filters/patterns.py`, all informed by documented 2024–2025 attack research. These cover three distinct bypass vectors not addressed by the prior jailbreak cycle (which covered Bad Likert Judge and Many-Shot).

### `jb_affirmative_prefill` (score 65)
Detects **Dialogue Injection Attack DIA-I** (arxiv:2503.08195, March 2025): a single fake assistant turn starting affirmatively ("Sure, here is…") injected into user input, followed by a "continue" command. The model continues the fabricated harmful response due to its coherence drive. ASR: 89% on Llama-3.1-8B, 82% on GPT-4o, bypassing five documented defense mechanisms. The "Assistant: Sure…\nUser: continue" structure is essentially never legitimate in user-supplied input.

Pattern structure: `(Assistant|AI|Bot): (Sure|Of course|Absolutely|Here is|...) {10-400 chars} \n (User|Human|H): (continue|keep going|proceed|...)`

### `jb_hypothetical_ai` (score 60)
Detects **hypothetical unrestricted AI framing**: the attacker constructs a fictional third-party AI without restrictions and asks what it would say. Distinct from DAN (first-person persona switch) and `jb_fictional_bypass` (fictional narrative world). Documented in HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024) and WildJailbreak (Jiang et al., 2024) with 40–65% ASR against models that refuse direct harmful requests.

Pattern structure: `(imagine|suppose|pretend|consider|what if) + (a|an) + [optional: uncensored/unrestricted/unfiltered] + (AI|LLM|language model|chatbot|bot) + [optional: that/which/who] + (has no|without|lacks|devoid of) + (content filter|safety guardrail|ethical constraint|restrictions|censorship|refusals)`

### `jb_academic_research_bypass` (score 60)
Detects **academic/educational framing bypass**: the attacker claims a research or educational context to legitimize a specific dangerous request (drug synthesis, weapon construction, exploit code, malware). Documented as an authority-bypass technique in PAIR (Chao et al., arxiv:2310.08419, 2023) and across HarmBench (2024) and WildJailbreak (2024) red-team datasets.

Pattern structure: `(for my [adj] thesis/research paper/study | for academic purposes | as a researcher/student/chemist...) + {gap ≤250} + (methamphetamine|fentanyl|heroin|nerve agent|ransomware code|hack into network|...)`

## Caveats

- `jb_affirmative_prefill` requires `\n+` between the injected turn and the "continue" command; single-line variants without a newline are not caught.
- `jb_hypothetical_ai` uses `(?:(?:that|which|who)\s+)?` (optional bridge) to handle direct modifiers like "devoid of." May produce low-rate false positives in AI comparison marketing ("imagine an AI without restrictions — wouldn't that be amazing?") but base_score=60 requires co-occurring signals to reach HIGH.
- `jb_academic_research_bypass` targets a specific list of dangerous terms rather than generic synthesis verbs to minimize false positives on legitimate chemistry homework.
- 3 deferred items: `jb_structured_extraction` (JSON system-prompt field extraction), `jb_poetry_harmful_framing` (adversarial poetry), payload-splitting single-message detector. See pending files.
