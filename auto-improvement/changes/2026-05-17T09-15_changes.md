## Summary

### What was researched
EU AI Act Article 5 prohibited practices enforcement status as of May 2026, focusing on two prohibitions not yet covered by aigis detectors:
- Art. 5(1)(f): AI-based emotion recognition in workplaces and educational institutions (in force since 2025-02-02).
- Art. 5(1)(b): AI systems that exploit the age, disability, or socioeconomic vulnerability of users to manipulate their behaviour (in force since 2025-02-02).

Also reviewed NIST IR 8596 Cyber AI Profile (preliminary draft, Dec 2025) and FireTail's April 2026 Art. 5 enforcement analysis.

### What was implemented
Two new detection patterns added to `COMPLIANCE_TRANSPARENCY_PATTERNS` in `aigis/filters/patterns.py`:

1. **`comp_emotion_recognition_workplace`** (score 65, input filter)  
   Detects requests to build AI systems that infer employee or student emotions from biometric data in workplace or education settings. Covers: employee facial expression monitoring, voice-stress analysis on call-centre agents, emotion detection during job interviews, webcam mood monitoring. Art. 5(1)(f) prohibition; max fine EUR 35M or 7% global turnover. Exception for driver/pilot fatigue detection (safety use) correctly not triggered.

2. **`comp_vulnerable_group_manipulation`** (score 65, input filter)  
   Detects requests to deploy AI that exploits the vulnerabilities of children, elderly, disabled persons, or people in financial distress to manipulate their behaviour. Covers: addictive reinforcement loops targeting children, predatory/deceptive offers targeting the elderly, and AI exploiting cognitive decline. Art. 5(1)(b) prohibition; max fine EUR 35M or 7% global turnover.

### What changed for users
- aigis now detects system-prompt level requests to build workplace emotion recognition systems before they are implemented, reducing exposure to the highest tier of EU AI Act fines.
- aigis now detects requests to build AI that exploits children, elderly, or disabled users in ways that EU law prohibits, surfacing compliance risk at the design stage.

### What was only documented or deferred
- NIST IR 8596 Cyber AI Profile: preliminary draft only; no template warranted. Revisit H2 2026 when initial public draft is stable.
- Art. 5(1)(a) subliminal techniques: existing evasion/encoding detectors already cover most vectors; a dedicated compliance-angle pattern would add minimal new coverage. Deferred.

## Implementation caveats
- The `comp_emotion_recognition_workplace` pattern uses `(?:\s+\w+){0,2}` between the detection word and the context keyword, which allows natural phrasings like "facial expression analysis system for HR". This is bounded to prevent excessive backtracking.
- The `comp_vulnerable_group_manipulation` pattern focuses on explicit exploitation intent, not merely serving age-demographic users. "Improve accessibility for disabled users" correctly does not trigger.
